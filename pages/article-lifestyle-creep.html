The Lifestyle Creep Trap

Earning more should improve your life. It should not automatically increase every recurring expense.

Lifestyle inflation is subtle because each upgrade feels reasonable in isolation. A better apartment. More subscriptions. More frequent delivery. A nicer car.

The problem is not spending more. The problem is allowing every raise to become permanent spending before you decide what the extra money is for.

Try a simple rule: when income rises, pre-assign part of the increase before your lifestyle absorbs it. More income can then buy assets, skills, experiences, or freedom — not just larger monthly obligations.

Progress is easier to see when your fixed costs grow more slowly than your income.

More income should create more choices, not only more bills.
